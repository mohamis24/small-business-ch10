Figure 10.1 • Role Architecture

Determining Types of Employees Needed

PART A Baseline Metadata

Identification of Job

Establishes the core administrative profile of the role within the small business:

  • Job title & department
  • Job code
  • Salary range
  • Assigned supervisor, etc.
PART B • THE ROLE Signed by Both

Job Description

Lists duties, responsibilities, working conditions, and organizational relationships:

  • a. Physical demands & minimum physical requirements.
  • b. Working & psychological conditions (relationships with people, money, equipment).
  • c. Summary of duties and responsibilities.
  • d. Days and hours of work.
  • e. Machines, tools, formulas, and equipment used.
Key Rule: Should always be reviewed and signed by both employer and employee to prevent misunderstandings.
PART C • THE PERSON Qualifications

Job Specifications

Detailed written statements of work assignments and mental, physical, and personal qualifications:

  • a. Educational background, knowledge, skills, techniques, and required experience/special training.
  • b. Personal characteristics such as sociability, articulateness, or aggressiveness.

Methods Used to Recruit Employees

Recruitment: Reaching out to attract applicants from which to choose one to fill a job vacancy.

1. Networking

Establishing and maintaining contacts with key persons in one's own or another organization as informal development or promotion systems.

2. Advertising

Includes ads in newspapers, Help Wanted signs in windows, radio, and other local media.

3. Internet / Website

Posting job openings online to reach a wider applicant pool quickly.

4. Employment Agencies

State agencies (in cooperation with U.S. Employment Service) and private agencies charging a fee and doing screening.

5. Employee Referrals

Present employees recommend a friend or acquaintance (while remaining aware of equal employment opportunity guidelines).

6. Scouting & 7. Temp Services

Scouting: Campus recruiting & professional orgs. Temp services: Private agencies providing part-time/temporary workers for a fee.

ACTIONABLE TACTICS

Small Business Recruiting Tips

  • ✓Ask current employees and offer finding fees.

  • ✓Consider internships—most universities can help.

Slides 10-16 & 10-17 • Hiring Pipeline

Selecting the Right Person for the Job

Selection: Involves choosing the applicant who has the qualifications to perform the job.

STEP 01 Screening

Gathering Information About the Applicant

Collecting applications, resumes, reference checks, test results, and conducting structured interviews to verify qualifications.

STEP 02 Decision

Making a Job Offer

Extending a formal offer to the most qualified candidate, clearly aligning on job description, duties, hours, and compensation.

STEP 03 Onboarding

Orienting the New Employee

Introducing the new hire to company policies, workspace, equipment, safety protocols, and team expectations.

Table 10.1 • Legal Compliance

10 Topics to Avoid When Interviewing Applicants

Click items to mark reviewed

1. Children: Do not ask whether they have children, plan to have children, or have child care.

2. Age: Do not ask an applicant's age unless job specific.

3. Disabilities: Do not ask whether the candidate has a physical or mental disability that would interfere with the job.

4. Physical characteristics: Do not ask for height or weight on an application.

5. Citizenship: Do not ask about citizenship (though IRCA requires verifying legal right to work in the U.S.).

6. Name: Do not ask a female candidate for her maiden name.

7. Lawsuits: Do not ask whether the candidate has ever filed a suit or claim against a former employer.

8. Arrest record: Do not ask applicants about their arrest records.

9. Smoking: Do not ask if they smoke (can lead to legal difficulties over ADA/health care cost fears).

10. AIDS and HIV: Never ask if candidates have AIDS or are HIV-positive (violates ADA & federal/state civil rights laws).

Preemployment Tests & In-Depth Interviews

TESTING STANDARD

Validity

Making sure that the test given actually relates or corresponds to job performance.

LEGAL RESTRICTION

Polygraph

An instrument for simultaneously recording variations in several different physiological variables.

In-Depth / Diagnostic Interviews

Detailed, probing, and penetrating preemployment interviews seeking to determine the applicant's character and other aspects of personality.

7 Rules for Improving the Interview Process

Rule 1 • Avoid

Do not ask the obvious questions.

Rule 2 • Avoid

Do not ask legally indefensible questions.

Rule 3 • Practice

Do ask the right questions, and be sure to know how to evaluate the answers.

Rule 4 • Caution

Rule 5 • Caution

Do not miss important clues.

Rule 7 • Balance

Do not rely too much on past credentials.

Training and Developing Employees

Key organizational and employee outcomes resulting from effective training:

01

Increased Productivity

Employees perform tasks faster, more accurately, and with greater confidence.

02

Reduced Turnover

Well-trained staff stay longer, lowering costly hiring and onboarding cycles.

03

Increased Earnings for Employees

Higher skill levels enable greater output and higher earning potential.

04

Decreased Costs Due to Errors

Lower waste of materials and reduced damage to equipment caused by mistakes.

05

Less Supervision Required

Frees up small business owners and managers to focus on strategic priorities.

06

Improved Employee Satisfaction

Boosts workplace morale, engagement, and sense of professional growth.

Ways of Training Nonmanagerial Employees

On-the-Job Training (OJT)

U.S. Department of Labor 6-Step Method

Has the worker actually performing the work under the supervision of a competent trainer:

Step 1: Telling workers what needs to be done.
Step 2: Telling them how to do the job.
Step 3: Showing them how to do the job.
Step 4: Letting them do the job under the trainer's guidance.
Step 5: Telling—not showing—them what they did right, what they did wrong, and how to correct the wrong activity.
Step 6: Repeating the process until the learners have mastered the job.

Apprenticeship Training

Blends hands-on On-the-Job Training (OJT) with structured learning of theory in the classroom, ideal for skilled trades and technical roles.

Internship Training

Combines practical On-the-Job Training (OJT) with academic learning at a cooperating school or college.

Cross-Training

Involves workers learning many job skills so they are more versatile, giving small businesses operational flexibility when staffing needs shift.

E-Training

Involves computer interaction with either specific software packages or specific online sites for scalable, self-paced employee training.
